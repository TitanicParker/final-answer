# Making 2017 Present

## Access, rectification and restriction as information tools

The GDPR strand is easiest to understand if it is not treated as a separate legal story pasted onto the clinical case.

Its importance is informational.

By 2025 the patient was trying to do three different things:

1. **see the record** that had governed the historical account;
2. **make the dispute visible inside that record**; and
3. **limit further use of disputed information while its accuracy and completeness were being challenged.**

Those aims map broadly onto access, rectification/annotation and restriction.

The legal validity of individual requests or institutional responses is for the competent regulator or court. This essay addresses the documentary function of the rights within the case.

## 1. Access changes the patient's epistemic position

The 19 March 2025 complaint expressly acknowledged incomplete access to internal notes, referrals and correspondence.

That fact matters. The patient had experienced the consequences of the clinical history without yet possessing the complete documentary architecture needed to reconstruct how the history had been made.

FOI and subject-access processes therefore did more than provide paperwork. They reduced the information asymmetry.

Access allowed the patient to compare lived memory with:

- diagnostic reasoning;
- treatment rationale;
- clinician-to-clinician correspondence;
- the discharge representation;
- later governance decisions;
- and the existence or non-existence of potentially corroborative records.

## 2. Rectification is not only about deleting historical opinion

One recurring institutional response is that a historic clinical opinion can remain accurate as a record of what the clinician believed at the time.

That proposition may be correct and still leave another question unanswered.

There are two distinct forms of accuracy:

> **Historical-opinion accuracy:** did the record accurately preserve the opinion that was held?

and

> **Event completeness / present contextual accuracy:** does the record, as used now, adequately communicate the relevant uncertainty, disagreement, later correction and present diagnostic position?

The patient did not need the institution to pretend that clinicians in 2017 knew the future. The deeper issue was whether a historically accurate opinion could continue operating as an unqualified present representation where important context was disputed or later evidence had materially changed the understanding.

## 3. The Protest makes completeness a separate issue

If the reported pre-discharge Protest is established, the data question becomes sharper.

A record could faithfully state that Parkinson's disease was diagnosed and that a treatment strategy was explained, while still be incomplete if it omitted a material contemporaneous objection that the principal disabling foot/gait problem remained unresolved.

That is why the case cannot be reduced to "opinion versus correction."

The possible defect is not necessarily that the historical diagnosis entry was false when made. It may be that the durable account did not preserve the full information state of the encounter.

## 4. Restriction addresses use, not historical truth

The patient's Article 18 position was directed at a practical concern: disputed personal data was continuing to be used to generate further institutional outputs while the underlying accuracy/completeness dispute remained unresolved.

The legal scope and effect of restriction in the specific circumstances are contested matters and should not be stated here as adjudicated breach.

But analytically the request reveals the problem clearly:

> **When disputed historical information is operationally reused, every new use can create another authoritative record.**

That is why the governance and data-protection strands converge.

## 5. Data topology becomes evidence

The access process also revealed where information was, or was not, held.

Requests relating to the reported bedside teaching interaction sought records concerning trainees, teaching, ward rounds and related material. Later responses said some relevant categories were not held.

That does not prove the Protest.

It does establish **asymmetric preservation**: the authoritative clinical account survives while some potential alternative traces are unavailable.

This is important because a surviving record naturally acquires evidential dominance when the source categories capable of testing it no longer exist.

## 6. The forward-only problem

Later governance correspondence raises a structural question that data rights make visible:

> **Can updated information be accepted prospectively while earlier institutional outputs remain untouched because they were generated from the information available at the time?**

Sometimes the answer may legitimately be yes. Historical records should not be falsified by rewriting them as though later knowledge existed earlier.

But there is a difference between preserving history and allowing an old representation to continue functioning without adequate qualification.

The real issue is whether later correction, annotation or contextualisation is sufficient to prevent historical material from being treated as unqualified present truth.

## 7. Genetics changes the present context

The February 2026 genetic result confirms Parkin-related Parkinsonism and dystonia.

This supersedes simplistic binary formulations that emerged during the developing 2025-2026 dispute. It does not erase the fact that Parkinson's disease was historically diagnosed, nor does it prove the legal outcome of any rectification request.

It does change the present contextual landscape. A current record that communicates only an idiopathic-Parkinson's formulation without the later PRKN/dystonia information would require careful consideration as to completeness and present meaning.

## 8. What the GDPR strand contributes to the case

The data-rights history does not prove the clinical case.

Its contribution is different.

It shows the patient trying to move from one side of the information asymmetry to the other:

> **to see how he had been represented, to place his disagreement beside the authoritative account, to identify what records survived, and to prevent disputed representations from silently generating further institutional facts.**

That is why the GDPR history belongs inside the overall reconstruction rather than at its edge.

The legal questions remain legal questions. The documentary significance is already clear.
