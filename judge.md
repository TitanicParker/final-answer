# The Case in One Page

The case does not require the Court to begin by deciding that Parkinsonism was wrongly diagnosed.

The documentary record supports genuine young-onset Parkinsonism, and later PRKN testing confirmed Parkin-related Parkinsonism and dystonia. The narrower question is whether the 2017 admission adequately resolved the relationship between the neurological syndrome and the disabling foot/gait problem before a durable institutional account was created.

The referral presented a combined problem: abnormal gait, bilateral foot problems, toe-clawing, increased tone, impaired movement and declining function. Neurology found strong Parkinsonian signs but also described the gait as partly dystonic, documented longstanding painful and structurally abnormal feet, and left the relationship "Not clear."

The record contains the information needed to ask a basic control question but does not visibly answer it:

> **What effect were painful and structurally abnormal feet having on the gait being interpreted neurologically, and what effect was the neurological disorder having on the feet?**

The patient's later evidence is that before discharge he objected that the feet remained unresolved and used an NCT / bald-tyres analogy. That evidence is not presently corroborated by a contemporaneous institutional note and must be proved. It is not inconsistent with his contemporaneous acceptance of a Parkinsonian diagnosis or his understanding of the treatment strategy. A patient can accept Parkinsonism while objecting that the principal disabling problem remains unexplained.

If the Protest occurred, was known to the relevant record-maker and was material, its absence from the completed account may have altered the information state transmitted forward. The durable record preserved Parkinson's disease, treatment rationale, Podiatry and patient understanding, but not the reported disagreement challenging whether that disposition was adequate.

The later case is therefore one of dependency, not presumed conspiracy. Subsequent clinicians could act reasonably and still inherit an information state that was more settled than the originating encounter. Repetition of that state is evidence of continuity; it is not automatically independent revalidation of the underlying proposition.

Later neurological evidence repeatedly re-entered through the feet: marked benefit on procyclidine, deterioration on withdrawal involving feet and gait, later dystonia/OFF-state discussion, and acknowledged improvement of the feet with Sinemet. These facts do not prove one mechanism for all foot pain. They do show that the neurological treatment state and the feet repeatedly interacted.

March 2025 is the epistemic turning point. Sinemet CR produced profound relief in the same bodily territory through which the patient had experienced the condition for years. His complaint of 19 March expressly acknowledged incomplete access to internal records. It was therefore notice before full discovery. FOI and later access allowed the patient to reconstruct the institutional history and compare it with lived memory and treatment response.

The propositions requiring determination are:

1. whether the Protest occurred substantially as remembered;
2. who heard it and whether the relevant author knew of it;
3. whether it was material to discharge and the foot/gait disposition;
4. whether its omission made the durable account materially more settled than the event;
5. which later decisions independently re-tested the disputed issue and which materially depended on inherited representation; and
6. what difference accurate recording and timely reconsideration would probably have made.

Medical breach and causation require expert evidence. Legal consequences require legal determination.

The central evidential principle is simple:

> **A later institutional decision cannot establish the completeness of its own historical inputs merely by relying on them.**
