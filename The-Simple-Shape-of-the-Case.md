# The Simple Shape of the Case

This case can be stated without beginning with negligence, conspiracy, or a dispute about whether Parkinsonism existed.

It begins with the feet.

The patient came to medical attention because he was struggling to walk, struggling to work on his feet, developing toe-clawing and bodily tension, and losing dexterity and ease of movement. The GP referral did not present two settled conditions. It asked whether an unusual stiff/festinant gait, bilateral foot problems, increased tone and worsening function were Parkinsonian or due to another underlying disorder.

Neurology found strong evidence of Parkinsonism. That part of the record is real. But the same admission also recorded a gait that looked partly dystonic, longstanding foot treatment, pes planus, hammertoes, corns, calluses and burning, and uncertainty about whether the leg and foot problems were separate, peripheral, or related. The relationship was left "Not clear."

The difficult question is therefore not whether the neurologist noticed the feet. He did. The difficult question is whether the record ever visibly integrated the feet with the gait he was interpreting.

The history already showed that the patient had needed foot treatment since 2009-2010 and that when he stopped bar work and spent less time standing, the foot pain reduced dramatically even while internal leg tension persisted. The clinician then used a "strikingly abnormal" gait as major neurological evidence while also documenting painful and structurally abnormal feet.

That makes one basic question unavoidable:

> **What effect were the painful feet having on the gait, and what effect was the neurological disorder having on the feet?**

Or, more simply:

> **How can anyone with sore feet walk normally?**

That question does not mean that sore feet explain Parkinsonism. It means that gait is produced through weight-bearing feet, and therefore the condition of those feet has to be integrated before the gait can be interpreted as though the two systems were independent.

The patient later says that this was, in substance, what he tried to protect before discharge. He recalls objecting that after an extensive neurological work-up the feet remained unresolved, and using an NCT / bald-tyres analogy: the car may have been examined, but the part meeting the road was still not fit.

The Protest matters because it was not necessarily a rejection of Parkinson's disease. The contemporary material indicates that the patient substantially accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis and understood why levodopa was being delayed. The remembered objection was narrower: **the principal disabling problem remained unresolved, and returning it to Podiatry did not answer the neurological question that had brought him into hospital.**

If that Protest occurred, it was an attempt to prevent the emerging institutional account from becoming more settled than the patient's own experience of the admission.

The durable record did not preserve that disagreement. It preserved Parkinson's disease as the primary diagnosis, the treatment strategy, Podiatry for the feet, and the patient's good understanding of why stronger dopaminergic treatment was being delayed.

That creates the central conditional proposition in the case:

> **If the Protest occurred, was known to the relevant record-maker and was material, its omission may have converted a contested transition into a record that travelled forward as substantially settled.**

This does not require later clinicians to have acted dishonestly. A record can alter the future simply because later people reasonably use it.

That is documentary dependency.

Once Parkinson's disease is the stable organising diagnosis and the feet are carried as pain, Podiatry, structure or possible neuropathy, later evidence arriving through the feet can be interpreted inside that arrangement. It does not necessarily travel backwards to reconstruct the original relationship.

That is what the later record repeatedly shows.

Procyclidine produced marked functional improvement. When it was stopped, the patient reported that his feet became restless and his walk began to fall apart. In 2018 Neurology itself documented rapid return of the outside-left-foot problem when procyclidine was withdrawn. In later years the record considered toe-curling, dystonia, OFF phenomena and pain related to dopaminergic state. By 2025 it was accepted that Sinemet made the feet feel better.

None of that proves that all foot pain was dystonia. It does show that the neurological treatment state and the feet were repeatedly interacting.

The difficulty is interpretive. If the feet have already been classified as mainly separate, medication effects appearing through the feet can be absorbed as pain modulation rather than forcing a reconstruction of the original foot/gait relationship.

The patient's body therefore knew things before he knew what they meant institutionally.

That is the second major feature of the case.

For years the patient possessed pain, gait difficulty, treatment effects and memory. The institution possessed the durable record, diagnostic coding, consultations, internal architecture and the representation that future clinicians would inherit.

This was not a simple secret. Much of the clinical reasoning was communicated. The important inequality was **record-making power**.

The institution knew how the patient had been represented to the future. The patient did not yet have the complete means to inspect that representation.

March 2025 changes that.

Sinemet CR produced profound foot relief. The importance of the event was not simply pharmacological. It occurred in the same bodily territory through which the patient had understood the condition for years. It therefore became a contradiction he could feel before he could fully explain it.

On 19 March he complained. That complaint is important precisely because it is incomplete. He expressly said that he did not have every internal note, referral or item of correspondence. He suspected anchoring, failure to reassess and possible hierarchical influence, and asked the hospital for review, disclosure, explanation and preservation.

The complaint is therefore **notice before full discovery**.

Afterwards the patient obtained more of the record through FOI and other access processes. He began to see the earlier treatment reasoning, the way the discharge account had been framed, the absence of the remembered Protest, the later governance record and the way later decisions relied on inherited information.

The sequence is:

> **bodily discovery -> complaint -> documentary discovery -> institutional reconstruction**

From March 2025 into early 2026, the patient progressively acquired the institutional half of the story. That is the restoration of informational symmetry.

It does not mean he acquired every record or that the institution agreed with him. It means he became increasingly able to place two histories side by side:

- the history he had lived in his body; and
- the history the institution had preserved and transmitted.

The February 2026 genetic result then prevented the case from collapsing into a simple right/wrong dispute. Two pathogenic Parkin variants were confirmed, and the specialist attributed both Parkinsonism and dystonia to PRKN disease.

That means Parkinsonism was real. It also means that a Parkinsonism-only reading was incomplete.

The case is therefore not:

> "They invented Parkinson's disease."

Nor is it:

> "They correctly diagnosed Parkinsonism, so nothing important remained unresolved."

It is:

> **A real Parkinsonian disorder was identified inside a presentation in which the feet remained the patient's persistent functional and informational signal. The record does not visibly resolve how painful feet and abnormal gait interacted. The patient later says he protested that unresolved state before discharge. If that Protest was materially omitted, a more settled representation may have travelled forward than the encounter actually contained. Later care may then have been partly shaped by that representation, while the patient lived the consequences without yet being able to inspect how his objection had been recorded. March 2025 began the process by which he finally could.**

That is the simple shape of the case.
