# CONFIDENTIAL DRAFT FOR LEGAL ADVICE

# Advisory Brief: Liam Moloney - Neurological Care, Foot Pain and Documentary Dependency (2017-2026)

**Prepared for:** Prospective solicitor advising Liam Moloney  
**Purpose:** Urgent preliminary assessment of clinical negligence, causation, limitation, preservation, appropriate defendants and expert evidence  
**Status:** Evidential case summary, not a pleading or expert opinion  
**Date:** 22 August 2026

## 1. Instructions sought

Liam Moloney seeks urgent advice concerning a prolonged course of disabling foot pain managed within Neurology from 2017 onward. The immediate questions are:

1. whether the 2017 investigation, discharge and treatment decisions arguably fell below the standard reasonably expected of a consultant neurologist;
2. whether later evidence of neurological and dopaminergic modification required explicit reconsideration of the original foot disposition;
3. whether earlier investigation or treatment would probably have avoided or materially reduced subsequent pain, disability and psychological injury;
4. who the proper proposed defendants are;
5. what independent expert disciplines are required;
6. what limitation dates may apply and whether protective proceedings or other immediate steps are required;
7. whether the alleged knowing omission of a material pre-discharge Protest is relevant to date of knowledge, concealment under section 71 of the Statute of Limitations 1957, causation, credibility or institutional dependency; and
8. what preservation, disclosure and witness steps should now be taken.

The proposed client understands that clinical negligence and causation cannot be established from treatment outcome alone and will require suitably qualified independent expert evidence.

## 2. Executive case theory

This is not best approached as a simple allegation that Parkinson's disease was misdiagnosed. Parkinsonism was clinically identified and subsequently supported by an abnormal DaTSCAN. The proposed case concerns the disabling feet: their insufficiently investigated relationship to the neurological syndrome, the treatment constraints imposed before that relationship had been resolved, and the failure of later neurological evidence visibly to reconstruct the original foundation.

Liam presented through his GP with painful feet, an abnormal gait which he attributed to that pain, toe-clawing, stiffness and inability to relax. During the July 2017 neurological admission, the feet remained a stated problem and the gait was described as suggestive of dystonic posture. The relationship between the feet and the neurological condition was nevertheless left "Not clear." On the presently available record, there is little visible investigation between the initial examination and discharge directed toward explaining why the dominant weight-bearing complaint remained unclear.

At the same time, a senior-backed policy of pronounced dopaminergic restraint was adopted. The documentary record refers to consultation with Professor Timothy Counihan and Neurology colleagues, states that ordinary dopaminergic treatment should be avoided for as long as possible, and records: "Myself and Tim are keen to hold back on Levodopa insofar as maybe possible unless you'd advise otherwise?" The discharge package also contained a Counihan-authored article describing juvenile/early-onset PRKN disease by reference to features including dystonia, symmetry and excellent levodopa responsiveness. The patient-specific synthesis considered PRKN but did not visibly integrate the dystonic-looking lower-limb posture into the unresolved feet.

Liam's evidence is that he protested before discharge: "Do you mean to tell me you're going to bring me in here, give me the full NCT, and send me out on bald tyres?" He recalls that the treating neurologist had earlier explained the senior treatment restraint by saying: "My boss told me not to give you the good stuff." The Protest is not independently recorded in the supplied institutional material. If accepted, it establishes that the neurologist was directly alerted before discharge that the principal complaint had not been resolved to the patient's satisfaction.

The post-Protest discharge synthesis presented a coherent, senior-supported and apparently understood course: established Parkinsonism, prolonged treatment restraint, a Podiatry pathway and a patient described as having good understanding. The Protest was absent. Later DaTSCAN confirmation was incorporated into the composite documentation. The suggested evidential concern is that a contested course was thereby capable of being inherited as an apparently accepted and sufficient foundation.

The subsequent record repeatedly gave the feet neurological significance. Procyclidine materially improved the patient's body, gait and feet; withdrawal produced a reported return of tension, restless feet and deterioration in walking. A later foot movement was described as "actually parkinsonian." The records later considered OFF symptoms and possible dystonia, documented worsening when medication had not been taken, and repeatedly accepted that Parkinson's medication modified the feet. Treatment was adjusted, but the supplied record does not show an express return to and independent reconstruction of the 2017 foot/Podiatry partition.

By 2023, the psychological effects were grave. Records described pain which "psychologically consumes him," walking as a "psychological mountain," and a life which had become "a lot smaller." An urgent GP referral stated that extreme burning pain was "causing death wish" and warned: "Despite his facade, I don't think he is doing well. I'd be grateful for your review before we get into trouble here."

On 10 March 2025, Liam described "murder in the feet," stated that his first 150 footsteps were "torture," and told the neurologist: "I won't live with it." The clinic record acknowledged that when Sinemet reached his system the feet felt better from a pain perspective. Sinemet controlled release was prescribed for nighttime/morning under-treatment. Liam collected it on 13 March and first experienced the resulting relief on waking on 14 March. The neurologist's later complaint response described the relief as "profound."

Liam submitted a formal complaint on 19 March 2025. It raised diagnostic anchoring, failure to reassess, possible hierarchical influence, prolonged preventable pain, psychological harm and loss of trust. It requested independent review, complete disclosure, explanation and preservation. It did not mention the 2017 Protest or allege concealment. Liam did not then possess or understand the documentary architecture later identified through the record production and institutional responses.

The neurologist's complaint response apologised, stated "I did not understand that this was how he felt," but also relied upon asserted continuity: "In essence we have known that [Liam] has Parkinson's; we have known it would be Sinemet or other dopamine agonist responsive." The response used earlier acknowledgements that levodopa might work very well and that clinicians might regret not starting sooner to support the deliberateness of the course. The concern is that continuity generated within the inherited framework was used to validate that framework without an independent reconstruction of the disputed hinge.

QPS referred the clinical-judgment complaint back to the treating neurologist. A later SIMT process reportedly concluded that there had been "no deficit in care." On the present materials, it is not evident that either process severed dependency on the originating synthesis, restored the alleged Protest to the chronology, obtained the missing decision-provenance material, or independently revalidated the foot disposition from the last uncontested evidential point.

The proposed primary formulation is therefore:

> A disabling, dystonic-looking and subsequently medication-responsive foot problem was not adequately investigated or integrated when central to the 2017 presentation. A material patient challenge before discharge was not preserved in the authoritative synthesis. Later neurological evidence generated prospective management changes but did not visibly reopen the originating disposition. Prolonged physical and psychological harm followed, culminating in documented death-related language and profound relief when targeted nighttime levodopa was introduced in March 2025.

## 3. Matters requiring independent expert determination

The following are proposed expert questions rather than concluded allegations:

### A. July 2017 standard of care

1. What assessment would a reasonably competent consultant neurologist have undertaken where painful feet and abnormal gait were central to referral and the gait appeared dystonic?
2. Was it reasonable to leave the neurological relationship "Not clear" at discharge on the investigation visible in the records?
3. Did the documented structural descriptions - including pes planus, hammer toes, corns and calluses - adequately investigate causation, or merely record physical findings?
4. Should movement-disorder expertise, gait observation/video, targeted dystonia assessment or another specialist assessment have been obtained?
5. Was Podiatry reasonable as one component of care, and if so, should it have been accompanied by a neurological reopening mechanism?

### B. Treatment restraint

6. Was the policy of withholding levodopa or dopamine agonists for as long as possible supported by the then-current standard of care for this young patient and suspected PRKN phenotype?
7. Did caution concerning long-term treatment complications justify the absence of a cautious, supervised diagnostic or therapeutic trial?
8. What did the early Procyclidine response and withdrawal deterioration mean clinically, and should those events have prompted reconsideration of the feet?

### C. Later reassessment

9. At what points did the longitudinal evidence require explicit reassessment of the 2017 partition?
10. Were the later foot movement, OFF-related pain, morning warming-up, medication timing effects, possible dystonia and dopaminergic responses clinically capable of impeaching the original account?
11. Was it reasonable to act on those matters prospectively without recording their retrospective significance?

### D. Causation

12. On the balance of probabilities, would earlier investigation, treatment or medication adjustment have materially reduced the pain and functional disability?
13. What proportion of the foot symptoms was probably neurological, dystonic, OFF-related, structural, peripheral, mixed or otherwise?
14. What, if any, inference can properly be drawn from the profound response to nighttime Sinemet CR?
15. What treatment period and injuries can medically be attributed to any identified breach rather than to the underlying disease?

### E. Psychological injury

16. Did the prolonged pain and treatment history cause or materially contribute to a recognisable psychiatric injury, suicidal ideation or exacerbation of an existing condition?
17. What risk was communicated by the 2023 "death wish" referral and the 2025 statement "I won't live with it," and what response would reasonable care have required?

## 4. The documentary hinge and alleged omission

The Protest should be treated as an evidential issue, not assumed to be institutionally corroborated. Its current support comprises Liam's direct evidence, the clinical circumstances giving the Protest inherent coherence, and the compatibility of its content with the unresolved foot issue. The necessary investigation includes:

- a detailed first-person statement identifying date, location, words, participants and immediate aftermath;
- identification of any witness, ward staff member, family member or person told contemporaneously;
- ward notes, progress notes, nursing notes, discharge-management records and audit trails;
- document creation, amendment and transmission metadata;
- Complex Case Meeting attendance, minutes, presentations and communications;
- direct Counihan communications and the scope of the advice actually given;
- any records of clinical-governance involvement before or after the Protest; and
- comparison of material added after discharge with material omitted despite occurring beforehand.

If Liam's evidence is accepted, the omission matters because the discharge document did not merely fail to reproduce a conversation. It omitted a challenge whose preservation would have shown that the adequacy of the foot disposition and discharge was contested. The immediate documentary effect was to permit understanding to be read as acceptance and uncertainty to coexist with operative closure.

Motive has not been proved. The proposed inference for investigation is narrow: recording the Protest may have required renewed examination, delay, express justification, treatment reconsideration or preservation of unresolved disagreement. The possible immediate reason for omission may therefore have been to avoid destabilising a senior-backed discharge course already substantially implemented. That inference must be tested against the author, metadata, ordinary documentation practice and alternative explanations.

No allegation is proposed that every later clinician shared any motive. If the omission was knowing and material, the later issue is dependency: conclusions materially reliant upon the apparent completeness, acceptance or sufficiency of the 2017 account cannot validate it merely by repetition. Independently established clinical findings remain evidence in their own right.

## 5. Potential legal architecture for advice

Subject entirely to expert and legal opinion, the material may require consideration of:

1. negligent failure adequately to investigate or formulate the foot/lower-limb presentation in 2017;
2. negligent adoption or application of an excessive treatment-restraint policy;
3. failure to establish a safe reassessment pathway for an unresolved disabling problem;
4. later failures to reconsider the original disposition when materially contrary evidence emerged;
5. failures concerning suicide-risk recognition, communication or response, if supported by psychiatric and clinical expert evidence;
6. causation of prolonged pain, disability, loss of amenity, economic loss and psychiatric injury;
7. the evidential and legal effect of incomplete or inaccurate clinical documentation;
8. possible vicarious and direct institutional liability;
9. any separate GP-related breach, contribution or causation issues;
10. the legal status and relevance of the QPS, complaint and SIMT processes; and
11. limitation, date of knowledge, concealment and any later discrete causes of action.

The complaint-handling and SIMT material should not automatically be treated as creating a fresh clinical-negligence cause of action or restarting time. Its principal present relevance may be notice, institutional knowledge, discovery, credibility, dependency and the adequacy of later governance review. Counsel should determine whether any later conduct is independently actionable.

## 6. Limitation: urgent protective assessment

This section identifies issues for advice and is not a concluded limitation opinion.

Section 7 of the Civil Liability and Courts Act 2004 generally applies a two-year period to personal-injury actions from accrual or the statutory date of knowledge, whichever is later. Section 2 of the Statute of Limitations (Amendment) Act 1991 defines the material date-of-knowledge elements. Section 71 of the Statute of Limitations 1957 postpones time in specified circumstances where a right of action has been concealed by fraud until discovery or discoverability with reasonable diligence.

The proposed date-of-knowledge analysis should distinguish:

- knowledge of pain and disability;
- knowledge that medication modified the feet;
- knowledge in March 2025 that targeted nighttime Sinemet CR produced profound relief;
- suspicion, expressed in the 19 March complaint, of anchoring, failure to reassess and hierarchy;
- lack of access at that stage to complete internal records;
- later receipt of SAR/FOI material;
- discovery that the alleged Protest was absent from the authoritative synthesis;
- discovery of the Counihan/PRKN/dystonia documentary collision;
- recognition that the complaint response relied upon continuity derived from the disputed foundation; and
- the later SIMT conclusion of no deficit in care.

The 19 March complaint is close to the present case in injury and general mechanism, but it is not coextensive with it. It expressly records lack of access to internal material and requests disclosure and independent investigation. It does not identify the Protest, knowing omission, documentary dependency or the use of inherited continuity as self-validation. Counsel should determine which material facts were required before Liam could reasonably know that the injury was attributable, in whole or part, to actionable conduct by an identified defendant.

Concealment should be maintained as a protective issue, not treated as a substitute for prompt proceedings. The evidence would have to justify more than an innocent omission or poor record keeping. The relevant questions include whether the Protest occurred, whether the author knew of it, whether it was understood to challenge discharge and the foot disposition, whether its exclusion was deliberate, and whether that exclusion concealed facts necessary to discover the right of action.

Continuing pain alone should not be assumed to produce a continuously moving limitation date. Counsel should separately consider whether later consultations or treatment decisions constitute discrete actionable breaches with their own accrual and knowledge dates.

**Immediate request:** advise without delay whether a section 8 notice, protective proceedings, standstill request, preservation demand or other formal step is required. The client does not wish to rely upon a contested concealment argument where limitation can instead be protected conventionally.

## 8. Evidential weaknesses and matters requiring candour

The following should be disclosed to the solicitor and experts at the outset:

1. The Protest is not presently independently corroborated by a native institutional entry or identified witness.
2. The words "My boss told me not to give you the good stuff" are Liam's recollection, not a documented quotation.
3. The evidence does not yet prove why the Protest was omitted.
4. The records do not presently establish exactly what Professor Counihan saw, knew or advised concerning the feet.
5. Parkinsonism and the abnormal DaTSCAN remain independently supported; the proposed case should not depend upon disproving them.
6. Dopaminergic response is clinically important but may not, without expert evidence, establish the sole cause of all pain.
7. Structural and peripheral findings may represent genuine contributors rather than false observations.
8. The 19 March complaint used formulations which now require refinement, including "independently trialed" Sinemet CR, categorical disproof of neuropathy, and an overbroad statement that dopaminergic responsiveness had not been tested.
9. The March 2025 clinical record appears to anticipate that additional Sinemet might relieve the experience of pain; any claim that the clinician predicted complete failure must be stated with exact evidential support.
10. Complaint handling and governance shortcomings may be powerful evidence without themselves sounding in damages as separate torts.

These limitations do not defeat the proposed case. They define what must be tested rather than asserted.

The first priority is a movement-disorders neurologist. The proposed pleadings should not precede expert support except to the extent counsel determines that protective proceedings are necessary and legally proper.

## 14. Closing assessment

The material presents a coherent and serious case for urgent legal and independent neurological assessment. Its strongest feature is not the eventual medication response in isolation. It is the longitudinal combination of an unresolved dystonic-looking foot problem central to presentation, a senior-backed treatment ceiling, an alleged material Protest before discharge, early and repeated neurological modification, escalating documented harm, profound relief from targeted nighttime levodopa, and an institutional response which appears to rely upon continuity without first establishing that the originating account was complete and independently valid.

The proposed case should remain disciplined:

> Motive belongs, if it can be proved, at the alleged omission. Dependency explains the potential effect of the resulting record. Clinical experts must determine breach and causation. The patient evidence establishes the missing conversations, observed conduct and lived consequences, but should be clearly distinguished from institutional documentation and independent corroboration.

The immediate priority is not further rhetorical development. It is to protect limitation, preserve evidence, obtain Liam's proof, and secure an independent movement-disorders opinion capable of determining whether the documented course supports a sustainable claim.
