# The Anatomy of the Omission

## The omission is important because of what the record had to represent

A discharge account does not have to contain every conversation. The issue is materiality.

If the patient did not protest, or if the reported exchange was immaterial to the disposition, its absence may prove little. But if he expressly objected before discharge that the dominant disabling foot/gait problem remained unresolved, and the relevant record-maker knew of that objection, then the completed account has to be judged against a different event.

The event would no longer be:

> diagnosis explained -> treatment strategy understood -> feet to Podiatry -> discharge.

It would be:

> diagnosis explained -> treatment strategy understood -> patient nevertheless disputes whether the feet have been resolved -> discharge proceeds.

That difference is the reason the omission matters.

## Omission as an information-state change

The strongest formulation does not begin with motive.

It begins with the information state transmitted forward.

The durable account preserved Parkinson's disease as the primary diagnosis, the rationale for treatment restraint, Podiatry involvement and the patient's understanding. It did not preserve the reported pre-discharge disagreement.

If the disagreement occurred, was known and was material, the surviving account may therefore have been **more settled than the encounter itself**.

That is an information-state change.

The effect is not merely archival. A hospital record is designed to travel. Later clinicians, administrators and reviewers do not inherit the bedside encounter directly. They inherit representations of it.

If a contested transition is represented as substantially accepted, the absence can alter what future readers perceive as requiring reconsideration.

## Why this does not require a downstream conspiracy

The omission theory becomes weaker, not stronger, if it assumes that everyone later shared an originating purpose.

No such theory is necessary.

Later clinicians can act carefully and in good faith while relying on the record available to them. A later reviewer can sincerely conclude that care was appropriate while using an information set shaped by earlier summaries. A data-protection decision can accurately record a historic professional opinion while not answering whether the underlying event was completely represented.

The mechanism is **documentary dependency**.

The relevant question for every downstream record is not "Was this person part of the omission?" It is:

> **Which propositions did this person independently establish, and which arrived already formed through the inherited record?**

## Compression and omission are related but distinct

The 2017 record already contains different levels of resolution.

The long neurology account is nuanced: Parkinsonism, atypical features, possible genetic disease, dystonic-looking gait, structural foot pathology, uncertainty about the feet, and a cautious treatment strategy.

The portable administrative layer is more categorical: Parkinson's disease, treatment plan, Podiatry.

That is documentary compression.

The reported Protest adds a different question. Compression may lose nuance because a summary is shorter. A material Protest, if established, concerns whether **disagreement itself** disappeared.

That is why the omission cannot be reduced to ordinary summarisation without first determining whether the disputed event occurred and mattered.

## The missing integrative question makes materiality stronger

The record had already gathered enough information to ask how painful feet affected gait and how the neurological disorder affected the feet. It did not visibly answer that question.

If the patient then objected that the feet remained unresolved, the Protest would have landed exactly on an uncertainty already present in the clinical record.

That would strengthen its materiality. It would not be an extraneous complaint introduced from outside the case. It would be a patient challenge directed at a relationship the clinician's own document had left "Not clear."

## The possible downstream effect

The effect of omission must be proved, not assumed.

But the mechanism to test is straightforward.

If the durable record presented the admission as more settled than it was, later evidence could be organised around that settlement. The feet could be managed as structure, pain, Podiatry or possible neuropathy. Parkinsonism could remain the principal neurological frame. Treatment effects appearing in the feet could be interpreted as modulation of pain within the established model instead of necessarily reopening the original relationship.

This is path dependence, not proof of error.

Later continuity may be genuine validation. It may be inherited continuity. It may be both. The task is to trace the sources.

## The later complaint does not retroactively prove the omission

March 2025 is important but must be dated correctly.

The patient experienced profound Sinemet CR relief and complained before he possessed the complete internal history. The complaint did not yet contain the mature omission-and-dependency theory. That later theory emerged through access to records, governance correspondence, missing-record responses and continued reconstruction.

This unfolding does not weaken the case. It protects it from hindsight.

The patient lived the contradiction before he understood the record architecture that might explain it.

## Motive belongs last

If evidence eventually establishes that the Protest occurred, that the responsible author knew of it, that it was material and that the final synthesis selectively excluded it, purpose may become a legitimate question.

But motive must be inferred from evidence, not built into the definition of omission.

The evidential sequence should remain:

> **event -> knowledge -> materiality -> omission -> altered representation -> dependency -> consequence -> only then, if supported, purpose.**

## The governing proposition

The omission theory is therefore narrow:

> **If a material pre-discharge Protest occurred and was known to the relevant record-maker, its absence may have converted a clinically contested transition into a more settled durable representation. The later significance lies not in assuming that every subsequent actor was wrong, but in determining which later decisions inherited that representation and whether they independently re-tested the unresolved issue.**

That is enough. The case does not need the omission to explain everything before it has first been proved to have changed something.
