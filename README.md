# The Record and the Choice Not Made

## An evidential case architecture

This repository examines a neurological admission in July 2017, the patient's reported pre-discharge Protest, the authoritative record created around that admission, the later clinical course, and the institutional response when the patient began reconstructing the history in March 2025.

It is not a pleading, a judicial finding, or a substitute for independent medical and legal opinion. Its purpose is to state the case coherently while preserving the distinctions between contemporaneous records, patient recollection, later clinical evidence, institutional interpretation, reasonable inference, and propositions that still require witnesses, metadata, disclosure, expert evidence, regulatory determination, or legal adjudication.

## The central architecture

The case did not begin with Parkinson's disease plus an incidental foot complaint. It began with a disabling presentation involving **feet, gait, toe-clawing, bodily tension, movement initiation, dexterity and function**. The GP referral asked whether the picture was Parkinsonian or another underlying disorder. Neurology then identified a strongly Parkinsonian syndrome, but the same record also described the gait as having a dystonic appearance, documented pes planus and hammertoes, noted longstanding treatment of the feet since 2009-2010, and left the relationship between the leg/foot symptoms and the neurological syndrome **"Not clear."**

The feet matter here for a reason deeper than symptom counting. Across the record they repeatedly functioned as the patient's most persistent **embodied information channel**: where standing and walking hurt, where gait was expressed, where treatment benefit and withdrawal deterioration became visible, and where the March 2025 Sinemet CR response finally forced a historical question back into view.

That does not prove that every foot symptom had one neurological mechanism. Structural foot pathology was real. Neuropathy, dystonia, OFF-state effects, pain amplification and mechanical loading can coexist. The point is narrower: **the feet could not safely be treated as informationally peripheral while gait was being used as a major neurological signal.**

The 2017 record itself therefore exposes an elementary integrative question that is not visibly answered:

> **What effect were chronically painful and structurally abnormal feet having on the gait being interpreted neurologically, and what effect was the neurological disorder having on the feet?**

In ordinary language:

> **How can anyone with sore feet walk normally?**

This is not a universal medical axiom. It is the plain-language form of a methodological question. The neurologist had already documented longstanding foot treatment, structural abnormalities, and the historical fact that foot pain reduced when occupational weight-bearing reduced. The information needed to ask the question was present. The surviving reasoning does not visibly establish the counterfactual gait: how this patient would walk if the feet did not hurt, or how the feet would behave if the neurological state changed.

## The Protest

The patient's later evidence is that, before discharge, he protested that the extensive neurological work-up had not resolved the feet, which remained the dominant disabling problem through which he understood the condition. He recalls using the analogy that he had been given the "full NCT" but was being sent out on "bald tyres."

The Protest is not best understood as rejection of Parkinsonism. The contemporaneous record strongly indicates that the patient substantially accepted the Parkinsonian diagnosis and understood the rationale for delaying levodopa or dopamine-agonist treatment. The potentially material distinction is different:

> **A patient can understand the Parkinsonian diagnosis and treatment strategy while still objecting that the principal disabling foot/gait problem has not been explained or resolved.**

If established, the Protest was therefore not merely another conversation. It was an attempted contemporaneous correction of the emerging institutional synthesis: an effort to prevent the feet from being displaced from the neurological question at the point of discharge.

The Protest remains later patient recollection. It is not presently independently proved by a contemporaneous ward entry or identified witness statement. Documentary silence neither proves nor disproves it. Its occurrence, hearers, timing, materiality, authorial knowledge and any purpose attached to omission must be established separately.

## The omission and the information-state change

The authoritative discharge account preserved a Parkinson's diagnosis, treatment strategy, Podiatry disposition and patient understanding. It did not preserve the reported disagreement that the feet remained unresolved.

If the Protest occurred, was known to the relevant record-maker and was materially omitted, the significance is not simply that one conversation was left out. The potential effect is an **information-state change**:

> **the patient attempted to preserve the unresolved status of the feet, while the durable record carried forward a more settled representation of the transition.**

That is the hinge between the clinical history and the later information history.

The institution possessed the machinery for converting lived disease into durable record: examination, imaging, diagnostic terminology, coding, consultation networks, discharge synthesis and administrative systems. The patient possessed his body, symptoms, treatment effects, function and memory of what he had said.

The decisive asymmetry was therefore not that clinicians simply knew more medicine. Much of the treatment reasoning was communicated. The deeper asymmetry was one of **forms of knowing and record-making power**.

The institution possessed the representation that later actors could inherit. The patient could live the consequences of that representation without yet being able to inspect how his unresolved objection had, or had not, been carried into it.

## Documentary dependency

The later record must therefore be read as a **dependency graph**, not as a stack of independent confirmations.

For each later record the relevant questions are:

- What did this author independently observe?
- What propositions were inherited from earlier records?
- Which primary materials were actually available?
- Where were the feet placed in the explanatory model?
- Did later evidence genuinely re-test the originating relationship, or was it absorbed inside the inherited frame?
- What new consequence did the later representation create?

This distinction matters because later clinical continuity can reflect independent validation, inherited information, or a mixture of both. Repetition proves continuity. It does not automatically prove independent revalidation of the original information state.

A later institutional process likewise cannot establish the completeness of its own historical inputs merely by relying on them.

## The feet continue to speak

The later clinical course repeatedly returned information through the same bodily territory.

Procyclidine produced striking functional improvement in 2017. During withdrawal the patient reported that his feet became restless and his walk began to fall apart. In 2018 the neurological record itself documented that, on stopping procyclidine, the first rapidly returning change involved the outside-left-foot phenomenon. Later records considered toe-curling, possible dystonia, OFF-state phenomena and pain related to dopaminergic state. By 2025 it was recorded that once Sinemet took effect the feet felt better.

These events do not prove that every foot symptom had a single central mechanism. Their significance is that the neurological treatment state and the feet repeatedly interacted. Once the feet had been placed into a secondary or separate explanatory category, however, medication effects appearing through the feet could themselves be interpreted inside that prior classification rather than necessarily reopening the original relationship.

The potential distortion is therefore not that observations were fabricated. It is that **their meaning could be conditioned by the frame into which they arrived.**

## March 2025: bodily discovery before documentary discovery

On 10 March 2025 the neurology record described severe longstanding foot pain and prescribed Sinemet CR. The patient says that after taking it he woke with profound relief. On 19 March he submitted a formal complaint alleging anchoring, failure to reassess and possible hierarchical influence.

That complaint is important because it expressly acknowledged that he did not possess every internal note, referral or item of correspondence. It is therefore **notice before full discovery**, not a completed statement of the later omission-and-dependency case.

The sequence matters:

> **body -> complaint -> institutional response -> FOI/archive access -> reconstruction**

By late May 2025 the patient stated that he had obtained internal materials through FOI. From there he increasingly acquired the institutional side of the history: earlier reasoning, record architecture, governance outputs, data-rights decisions, missing-record responses and the relationship between the original clinical record and later institutional processes.

## Restoration of informational symmetry

The period from March 2025 into early 2026 is therefore not merely a complaint chronology. It is a chronology of **progressive restoration of informational symmetry**.

The patient of 2017 must never be given the knowledge of the investigator of 2026. The same rule applies within 2025. What was experienced in the body often preceded understanding of its documentary significance.

By January 2026 the patient possessed enough of the institutional history to place the lived case beside the recorded case and examine how they diverged. That does not mean that patient and institution agreed, or that the patient obtained every record. It means that the earlier imbalance in access to the institutional representation had materially narrowed.

The February 2026 genetic result then imposed a final guardrail against binary retrospective narratives: two pathogenic Parkin variants were confirmed, and the specialist attributed **both Parkinsonism and dystonia** to compound heterozygous PRKN mutations.

That later evidence means neither "Parkinsonism was invented" nor "the 2017 account was complete." Genuine Parkinsonism was recognised. The unresolved question is how the feet, dystonic phenomenology, structural pathology and treatment-responsive features were integrated before the record acquired durable authority.

## Governance and the forward-only problem

The complaint, incident-review, data-protection and later governance record adds another layer.

Later processes may have acted reasonably within the information available to them. But if those inputs descended materially from an earlier representation, the validity of a later conclusion does not itself prove the completeness of the historical foundation.

The recurring systems question is therefore:

> **Can new information be accepted prospectively while the historical foundation that organised the earlier course remains insulated from retrospective reconstruction?**

This repository uses terms such as documentary dependency, path dependence and recursive validation to identify that risk. They are analytical tools, not self-proving findings about every later decision.

## What is established, and what still has to be proved

The repository does **not** claim that every later clinician, complaints officer, reviewer or data-protection actor was negligent, dishonest or motivated by an originating omission.

Nor does it claim that every foot symptom was dystonia, that Podiatry was inherently inappropriate, that the DaT scan proved idiopathic etiology, that Sinemet CR disproved Parkinsonism, or that later genetics retrospectively determines the standard of care in 2017.

The load-bearing propositions that still require proof include:

- whether the reported Protest occurred substantially as remembered;
- who heard it;
- whether the relevant record-maker knew of it before finalising the durable synthesis;
- whether it was material to discharge and the foot/gait disposition;
- whether its absence made the surviving representation materially more settled than the encounter actually was;
- which later decisions materially depended on that representation rather than independently re-testing the issue;
- and what difference accurate recording and timely reconsideration would probably have made.

Medical breach and causation require independent expert evidence. Legal and GDPR consequences require appropriate legal or regulatory determination.

## The central proposition

The strongest present formulation is therefore:

> **The case is a history of embodied information becoming institutional representation. The patient repeatedly experienced the condition through his feet. Real Parkinsonism was identified, but the record does not visibly resolve the reciprocal relationship between painful feet and observed gait. If the reported Protest is established, it was an attempt to stop that unresolved relationship being lost at discharge. If a material Protest was then omitted, the attempted correction itself failed to travel forward, and later actors could inherit a more settled information state than the patient remembers experiencing. From March 2025 onward, bodily response, archival access, governance records and later clinical evidence progressively allowed the patient to compare institutional memory with lived memory and restore informational symmetry.**

That architecture is the organising principle of the repository.
