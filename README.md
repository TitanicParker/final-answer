# The Record and the Choice Not Made

## An evidential case architecture

This repository assembles a series of essays concerning a neurological admission in July 2017, the patient’s reported pre-discharge Protest, the discharge account created after it, the later clinical history, the human consequences of that history, and the institutional response when the patient complained in March 2025.

It is not a pleading, a judicial finding or a substitute for independent medical and legal opinion. Its purpose is to state the case coherently while maintaining the necessary distinctions between:

- contemporaneous institutional documentation;
- clinician-recorded patient reports;
- the patient’s direct recollection of spoken events;
- reasonable inference from chronology and selective documentation;
- counterfactual reconstruction used to illuminate a missed opportunity; and
- matters which remain dependent upon witnesses, metadata, disclosure and expert evidence.

## The central architecture

The patient entered the 2017 admission with a unified disabling problem involving his gait, feet, toe-clawing and bodily tension. Parkinsonism was identified, but the relationship between the feet and the neurological syndrome remained expressly “Not clear,” while the observed lower-limb posture appeared dystonic. The admission therefore did not begin with two established conditions. It began with one presentation whose internal relationship required resolution.

The Parkinsonism inquiry moved quickly through examination, imaging, laboratory investigation, video, multidisciplinary discussion, genetic consideration, treatment planning and administrative certification. The accessible record does not demonstrate comparable forward movement in relation to the feet after the initial history and examination. Structural findings were listed, and peripheral separation was entertained, but the surviving documentation does not show the serial assessment or patient-specific reasoning bridge by which the dominant complaint moved from dystonic-looking uncertainty to an operative Podiatry disposition.

A senior-supported treatment architecture was nevertheless established. Ordinary dopaminergic treatment was to be withheld for as long as possible, while the feet passed operationally toward Podiatry. Yet the same documentary package recorded the dystonic-looking posture, raised PRKN/PARK2 and enclosed Professor Counihan’s own article connecting juvenile PRKN disease with dystonia, symmetry and excellent levodopa responsiveness. The record contained the observation, the relevant hypothesis and the specialist knowledge capable of connecting them, but did not visibly allow that connection to govern the patient-specific treatment of the feet.

Before discharge, the patient says that he challenged this unresolved partition. His Protest did not reject the Parkinsonism diagnosis. It asserted that the dominant disabling problem remained unexplained and that returning him to a Podiatry pathway which pre-dated the admission did not constitute clinical resolution. He recalls using the analogy that the institution had given him the “full NCT” but was sending him out on “bald tyres.” If established, the Protest was material to the adequacy of discharge, the meaning of the foot disposition, the completeness of the treatment plan and whether the patient accepted the course attributed to him.

The authoritative discharge synthesis did not preserve that challenge. Instead, it recorded a clear senior-backed strategy, Podiatry, “good understanding” and a plan incorporated “as per above.” The document remained open long enough to absorb the post-discharge DaTSCAN as expected confirmation, but not the pre-discharge Protest as evidence that the plan had been contested. If the Protest was knowingly omitted to avoid reopening an already commissioned discharge course, that omission converted a contested decision into an apparently accepted foundation.

The repository does not treat that motive as proved. The Protest remains the patient’s direct evidence and presently lacks independent corroboration in a native institutional entry or identified witness statement. Knowledge, materiality, purpose and causation must be established separately. The narrower proposition is that, if knowing material omission is established, the resulting documentary environment cannot authenticate itself merely through the later repetition of propositions descended from it.

Later clinicians encountered the completed foundation rather than the unresolved bedside state that preceded it. They could act reasonably and in good faith while still working inside an altered decision environment. The feet could be treated as pain, pressure, neuropathy, Podiatry or a separate peripheral problem; medication could be adjusted prospectively; and recurring symptoms could be managed locally without anyone being assigned responsibility for asking whether the recurrence impeached the original partition. No downstream conspiracy was required. Documentary dependency was sufficient.

Neurological evidence nevertheless returned repeatedly through the feet. Procyclidine improvement and withdrawal deterioration affected the feet, tension and gait. Later records described the foot movement as Parkinsonian, documented toe-curling, possible dystonia and OFF-related pain, and connected walking failure with inadequate levodopa effect. By 2025, Parkinson medication was acknowledged to relieve the feet, additional Sinemet was expected to provide further relief, Sinemet CR was prescribed specifically for the severe morning foot problem, and the resulting relief was described as profound. These events generated treatment changes, but the supplied record does not show them travelling backwards to reconstruct the 2017 foundation.

The spoken record adds a separate evidential layer. The patient recalls being told after the Complex Case Meeting, “My boss told me not to give you the good stuff.” That wording is not preserved institutionally, but its substance is consistent with the documented senior provenance of treatment restraint. By contrast, the 2021 statement that DBS “is only ever for the tremor element” is preserved in the neurologist’s own signed letter. The patient says that this information still governed his understanding when a different specialist described him as a good DBS candidate in December 2025. The repository identifies these sources precisely rather than treating every quotation as having the same status.

The patient experienced more than delayed symptom relief. He was required to live and seek care within a history that did not preserve the moment when he had challenged the unresolved problem. The institution possessed the authoritative account; the patient was left to carry the omitted disagreement in his memory and the unresolved question in his body. Each recurrence required him to explain again what the foundational record made appear settled, while the passage of time increased the authority of the written account and weakened his ability to recover the omitted state.

The consequences ultimately reached documented language of death. In 2023, an urgent GP referral described the bilateral foot and lower-leg suffering as extreme and “causing death wish,” expressed concern that the patient was not doing well and requested review “before we get into trouble here.” In 2025, the neurological record described “murder in the feet,” the first 150 footsteps as “torture,” and recorded the statement “I won’t live with it.” This supports a two-year span of suicide-relevant documentation associated with the unresolved foot suffering, although it does not by itself prove uninterrupted suicidal intent throughout that period.

The final clinical encounter and complaint form a critical sequence. Sinemet CR was prescribed on 10 March 2025. The patient says that he collected it on 13 March and first woke with profound relief on 14 March. He submitted his formal complaint on 19 March. The complaint alleged anchoring, failure to reassess and possible hierarchical influence, described prolonged preventable pain and psychological harm, and requested independent review, disclosure, explanation and preservation. It expressly acknowledged that the patient did not possess all internal material. It was therefore notice of injury and suspected mechanism before full documentary discovery, not a completed statement of the later omission-and-dependency case.

When the patient complained, the response acknowledged that his pain and discomfort had been documented but stated that the clinician had not understood that this was how he felt. It did not visibly reconstruct the earlier death-wish warning as part of the causal and governance history. Instead, it relied upon the longitudinal record to show that Sinemet responsiveness had long been understood, that treatment restraint had been deliberate, that the patient had received Sinemet since 2021 and that the eventual profound relief fitted the established approach.

That created the decisive asymmetry. The record’s continuity was treated as sufficiently reliable to demonstrate institutional knowledge, foresight and a coherent treatment strategy, but not given equivalent effect as evidence that the patient’s suffering had escalated to recorded death-wish language or that the original unresolved partition required reconstruction. Continuity supported the institution prospectively and defensively; contradiction and patient danger were not permitted the same retrospective force.

The complaint process therefore risked becoming the final tooth of the ratchet. Later care conducted within the inherited framework was used to validate that framework, even though its apparent continuity may have been a product of documentary descent rather than independent reconsideration. The future generated by the foundation was treated as evidence that the foundation had been sound, instead of being examined as evidence of the foundation’s reach and consequences.

The post-clinical record now carries that inquiry beyond the first complaint response. QPS classified the matter through a clinical-judgment route and used the treating clinician as the principal explanatory source. The patient attempted to preserve a counter-record through rebuttal, Stage 2 requests, incident-management correspondence and continuing written evidence. SIMT later concluded that there had been “no deficit in care.” The available outputs do not visibly demonstrate a source-by-source reconstruction beginning before the 2017 synthesis or a dependency analysis capable of distinguishing later independent support from propositions inherited from it.

The patient then used GDPR access, rectification and restriction rights in an attempt to make the historical dispute operationally visible. Article 16 requests sought correction or annotation; Article 15 sought peripheral traces of the bedside teaching encounter; Article 18 attempted to prevent disputed data from generating further authoritative outputs without qualification. These routes did not themselves prove the Protest or invalidate the historical opinions. They exposed a second systems question: whether an institution can preserve the fact that an opinion was historically held without allowing that opinion to continue operating as unqualified present truth after materially contrary information emerges.

The later governance record supplies the clearest expression of the forward-only rule. Following a reported December 2025 dystonia diagnosis, QPS stated in January 2026 that updated diagnoses may supersede earlier working diagnoses clinically without requiring prior governance findings or historical records to be reopened. The institution was therefore not merely displaying resistance to backward reconstruction implicitly. It articulated a position under which the present may change while the validity of the institutional past remains protected by the information state and process existing at the time.

The clinical record thus became the substrate of a governance record. Complaint closure, SIMT, data-protection decisions and later QPS correspondence added new layers of institutional authority while the completeness of the originating foundation remained disputed. This is recursive validation: the system no longer merely depends upon the original account; it generates fresh institutional outputs from that dependency and then possesses additional outputs capable of supporting closure.

The case is accordingly not that every later clinician, complaints officer, reviewer or data-protection actor was negligent, dishonest or motivated by the original omission. It is that a material patient challenge may have been knowingly omitted at the precise moment when recording it would have required reconsideration of a senior-backed discharge plan. That act, if proved, created an apparently uncontested foundation; later care became materially dependent upon it; neurological evidence repeatedly returned without visibly reopening it; the patient’s suffering escalated to suicide-relevant documentation; and later clinical and governance processes generated additional authority without first independently revalidating the foundation from which their historical information descended.

The central proposition is therefore this:

> Motive belongs at the omission; dependency explains the subsequent record; injury belongs to the patient; and circularity appears when the institutional future produced by the omitted Protest is relied upon as proof that the originating course was coherent. If knowing omission is established, later dependent conduct cannot authenticate the foundation merely through repetition. It must be severed from that foundation and independently revalidated from the last reliable point before the Protest disappeared.

## Governing evidential limits

- The Protest must be proved; documentary silence does not prove it, but neither does it disprove positive patient evidence.
- Motive for omission is an inference requiring evidence, not a starting assumption.
- The record does not establish that Professor Counihan personally knew of or advised upon every disputed foot feature.
- Podiatry was not inherently inappropriate; the issue is whether it was allowed to stand in place of integrated neurological resolution.
- Dopaminergic response is clinically significant but does not, without expert evidence, establish the sole mechanism of every component of pain.
- Later clinicians need not have shared knowledge or motive for their decisions to have depended materially upon an inherited account.
- Documented death-related language establishes seriousness and notice, not uninterrupted suicidal intent throughout the entire period.
- The counterfactual conversation is not presented as an event which occurred. It illustrates what candour, ownership and a genuine reopening mechanism might have looked like.
- Medical breach, legal causation and recoverable damage require independent expert and legal analysis.
